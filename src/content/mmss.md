Founded in 1978, Northwestern’s Mathematical Methods in the Social Sciences program is a selective, honors-level major that blends quantitative courses in mathematics, economics and data science with social sciences like political science and psychology.

“MMSS is a program that creates problem solvers,” said MMSS Program Director and Economics Prof. Ivan Canay.

Although it was created to prepare students for graduate programs, most undergraduates today find private-sector postgraduate jobs in industries like consulting, finance and technology upon graduation. According to program coordinator Nicole Schneider, the program currently has 135 to 150 students. Around 35 seniors graduate every year.

Canay said the MMSS program teaches students to answer questions about different fields analytically and rigorously.

Dylan Yan (Weinberg ’25) found out about the MMSS program while applying to college in high school. At the time, his interest in economics and consideration of pursuing graduate studies in the field motivated him to apply to NU.

Yan now works in economics and litigation consulting at Cornerstone Research, which he said is like “doing math for lawyers.” His responsibilities consist of everything from drafting reports to informing expert witnesses for class action lawsuit settlements.

“I felt like some of MMSS’ courses taught me the technical stuff pretty well,” Yan said. “But also how to write that up into an explainable report.”

In particular, Yan said writing his MMSS senior thesis helped him hone his ability to explain technicalities and defend his assumptions. All MMSS students are required to design and execute a research project on the topic of their choice, under the close supervision and guidance of faculty during their final year at the University.

Weinberg junior Grant Kahler said he chose the MMSS program because he knew he wanted to do a math-related program in college, but wasn’t sure he wanted to pursue pure math.

As an adjunct major, the program requires students to meet the requirements of another approved major in addition to the MMSS degree. Kahler is also majoring in economics with a minor in data science. He said he chose to study economics because MMSS courses allow him to pass out of several required courses for the economics major.

Kahler said he only has to take six classes to complete the economics major because there are many introductory classes he can pass out of having done MMSS. The economics major consists of 14 course requirements.

He added that the MMSS program not only allows students to become friends with their peers, but also sets them up with pre-professional connections, making it a rewarding experience.

“I’ve definitely learned a lot overall, and I really love the program,” Kahler said. “I think not a lot of people know about it before they come to Northwestern, and a lot of people come in wishing that they had applied.”

As a junior, Kahler is no longer taking MMSS courses, but his senior thesis remains a requirement to graduate from the program.

Weinberg sophomore Miki Hayashi started off as a pre-med neuroscience and economics student. However, taking a math sequence class made her “(fall) in love with the environment.” Hayashi said if she had known about the program her first year, she would have applied to it.

“It’s such a tedious and intensive major that only the people who are willing to search it by themselves find more about it,” Hayashi said.

As a student from Tokyo, Hayashi said the MMSS program allows students to form closer relationships with professors in a small classroom setting.

Canay said students who love the content of MMSS courses should stay. He advised students to remind themselves that the program is about struggling, getting better and adjusting.

“You need to develop resilience by facing challenges,” Canay said. “And I think MMSS is proud of that.”
