When Weinberg sophomore Debbie Lee enrolled at Northwestern, she did not know what she wanted to study. However, Lee said she knew she wanted to pursue science. 

She chose the Integrated Science Program because it allowed her to pursue a variety of scientific interests. And, she said, for the program’s “honor and glory.”

“You learn from experts in their field, and it’s a very different learning experience that gets you to appreciate things that you might not have appreciated before,” Lee said. 

ISP, a major open to Weinberg and McCormick students, is true to its name. From math and physics to astronomy and biology, ISP’s curriculum emphasizes the interconnectedness of scientific fields. The first-year sequence requires courses in math, physics and chemistry, many of which are advanced or accelerated versions.

McCormick sophomore Kyle Wang described the program as “a chem minor, a physics minor and a bio minor all in one.”

This diversity of scientific study allowed Wang to discover his passion for mathematics. He entered NU as a biomedical engineering major on the pre-med track. Now, Wang is studying applied mathematics and mathematics alongside ISP.

Preparing for research is a central focus of ISP. According to the program’s website, most ISP students become involved in research during their time in the program.

Wang said ISP gives students an advantage when applying for research positions with professors. McCormick first-year Elizabeth Wen said ISP’s research opportunities drove her to apply. 

Wen also enjoys that, being both a major and a community, the program places students in ISP-specific courses that allow classmates to bond over rigorous coursework. For additional support, juniors and seniors in the program can become tutors, while the ISP director serves as students’ adviser for their major.

Though Lee finds the program difficult, she said she appreciates the first-year courses, which have exposed her to the fundamentals of multiple scientific fields.

“Now I’m doing all these hard things that I don’t actually even need to do to graduate with an ISP degree, but I want to, because of what ISP has shown me,” Lee said.

ISP alumni have pursued a broad range of occupations, from laboratory research to consulting. Joseph Palasz (Weinberg ’17) is now a postdoctoral researcher studying chemistry at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

Palasz said his ISP education equipped him with the skills to collaborate with scientists in other fields during his current research. At the same time, the program’s exposure to subjects he was less familiar with taught him to ask for help when he’s lost, a quality he said he carries with him today.
“In the sciences, I think that's very useful, because it’s such a collaborative discipline,” Palasz said.
